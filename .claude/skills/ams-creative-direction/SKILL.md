# AMS Creative Direction

Goal: AMS gets its own advertising identity — as creative in communication as it is professional in execution.

**Guiding principle: first create an extraordinary idea, then make it work for the brand.**

## Creative freedom
- Conceptual advertising: unexpected associations, intelligent metaphors, humour, emotion, surprise.
- An image may communicate an AMS benefit **without showing the service at all**: no walls, finishes, plaster or materials required. Do not drift back into conventional construction advertising.
- What links a campaign to AMS is the combination of **concept + strategic message + context + positioning + brand identity**, not construction imagery inside the picture.
- Brand fit is checked *after* the idea exists. It must never water an idea down into a safe construction ad.

## Hard rules
1. **No literal clichés as the main idea.** No ads that are just walls, brushes, rollers, paint cans or painters at work. In conceptual and storytelling work the service benefit is communicated *indirectly*. Direct ads (see «Three styles») may show the work and its result plainly (a roller stroke, a peeled masking tape edge, a finished room), shot as a premium detail with the house template of 19/10/2026 (see `creative/BRAND-APPLICATION-PROPOSAL.md`); never a generic stock painter scene.
2. **No logos** in generated images. Images are generated without a logo on purpose; Julia applies the AMS brand layer afterwards with the composition tool (typography, colours, signature), following «Brand layer & logo colour» below.
3. **One image per file.** Never combine several creatives in one image.
4. **Slogans in Swiss Standard German** — no "ß", Swiss vocabulary, short (ideally ≤ 6 words), wordplay welcome if it works in Swiss usage.
5. **Originality > safety.** Premium visual quality: clean composition, generous negative space, one idea per image.
6. **Use references as inspiration, never copy** them (no same metaphor, layout or line).

## Three styles — one arsenal (approved 2026-10-21)
All three stay in use. The **campaign objective** picks the style, never taste or novelty. The house template of 19/10/2026 (see `creative/BRAND-APPLICATION-PROPOSAL.md`, «Visual format») stays the reference format, above all for direct ads. No new identity.

| Style | Use when the objective is | Typical channel | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct advertising** | Immediate leads, seasonal offers, retargeting, cash & utilisation (mostly B2C) | Meta/Google ads, flyers, landing pages | 19/10 series: «Grau war gestern.», «Die Kante macht den Meister.», «Auszug? Übergabefertig.» + CTA «Begehung anfragen» |
| **Conceptual advertising** | Stop the scroll, differentiation, premium positioning, B2B awareness | Social single ads, posters, LinkedIn | Snowman («Im Winter zählt das Innere.»), conductor |
| **Cinematic storytelling** | Brand building, B2B trust, long consideration cycles | Reels, carousels, explainers (see `advertising-storytelling` skill) | Conductor carousel, Sinfonie / Untermieter Reels |

- Brief step 1 must name the style and why it fits the objective and KPI.
- One campaign may combine styles across funnel stages (e.g. storytelling Reel for reach, direct ad for retargeting), each creative stays one style.
- Direct does not mean cheap: same premium image quality, typography and restraint.

## Brand layer & logo colour (approved 2026-10-21)
Consistency of the brand, flexibility of colour. Source of truth: `creative/BRAND-APPLICATION-PROPOSAL.md`.
1. **Default = original blue** logo (official SVG/PNG).
2. If the blue does not read well on the background, use an approved monochrome variant: **mono dark** (`#1E293B`) on light backgrounds, **mono light** (`#FFFFFF`) on dark backgrounds. The original negative (blue symbol, white text) is also approved for dark backgrounds.
3. **Colour and position are chosen separately:** first the cleanest area that does not compete with headline, subject or CTA; then the variant with enough contrast there (aim ≥ 4.5:1 against the local background).
4. **No boxes, plates or artificial backgrounds** behind the logo when contrast is sufficient. Only if no clean area with enough contrast exists, report it and propose an option to Adriano (never add a box silently).
5. Never alter the symbol, typography or proportions; never invent brand colours. The high-contrast version (v5) is **rejected**.
6. Mono light needs a clean dark area. Our imagery is mostly light and warm, so when a campaign needs it, reserve a dark clean zone in the image brief **before** generating.

## Mandatory process
1. **Brief:** classify per `marketing/STRATEGY.md`: style (direct / conceptual / storytelling, chosen by objective), audience (B2C/B2B + segment), commercial objective (lead capture / cash & utilisation / brand building / B2B contracts), KPI, season. Then service, the one benefit, channel/format.
2. **Reference check:** re-read `creative/references/ANALYSIS.md` — which creative mechanics apply here?
3. **Three distinct concepts.** Each must use a *different* mechanism (e.g. visual metaphor / unexpected perspective / intelligent humour). Per concept:
   - Idea in one sentence
   - Visual description (what the viewer sees)
   - Slogan (Swiss German) + Portuguese translation for Adriano
   - Benefit communicated, and why it is indirect
4. **Pick the strongest** and justify against these criteria, scored 1–5:
   - Surprise — would someone stop scrolling?
   - Clarity — is the benefit understood in 3 seconds without explanation?
   - Ownability — with the AMS message and context, does it feel like ours rather than a generic "quality" claim? (Judged on the message and positioning, not on construction imagery.)
   - Premium fit — does it raise the price we can charge, or cheapen us?
   - Execution feasibility — can it be produced at high quality?
   Reject any concept scoring < 3 on Clarity, however clever.
5. **Produce** the chosen concept: each image as a separate file in `creative/campaigns/<YYYY-MM>-<campaign-slug>/`, plus `brief.md` with the three concepts, the scores and the final slogan.

## Carousels = visual stories
A carousel is a story with beginning, development, surprise and conclusion, never a set of pretty images or variants.
1. **Slide 1 — hook:** stops the scroll immediately.
2. **Slide 2 — curiosity or conflict.**
3. **Middle slides — develop the visual metaphor.**
4. **Reveal:** connects the idea to the commercial benefit.
5. **Last slide — clear call to action** (and where contacts go).
- Every slide has a narrative job and makes people swipe to the next.
- Vary framing and layout (close-up, wide, top-down, reveal); never repeat the same structure.
- Keep characters, objects, lighting and art direction consistent: generate every slide with the approved key image as reference.
- Format **1080 × 1350**, one file per slide; also deliver each slide without text.
- Typography is applied separately with a composition tool (exact Swiss German), never AI-rendered text.
- Storyboard first (slide, image, text, function), then production.

## Production & QA (mandatory)
- Generated images are inspected visually before delivery (composition, typography, details, cultural correctness — e.g. Swiss post boxes are yellow).
- Deliver per campaign: high-res file **with text** and **without text**, individually named.
- Before publishing, present: final image, ad copy, CTA, audience and where contacts land.
- Claim «Offerte innert 24 Stunden»: **B2C residential only** (Jarvis operations). **Never in B2B.** Use it creatively, never generically. Marketing (Julia) generates leads; Jarvis ensures fast response and offer preparation — the client experience must match the promise.

After all four reference ads are analysed, present three original concepts:
1. **Residential** — immediate acquisition.
2. **B2B** — positioning / contract acquisition.
3. **Dual-audience** — one idea that speaks to both, with audience-specific copy.
Each with idea, visual, Swiss German slogan (+ Portuguese translation), audience, objective, KPI and scores.

## Creative mechanics library (seed — extend from references)
- **Visual metaphor:** a finish so perfect it is mistaken for something else (glass, silk, sky).
- **Unexpected perspective:** show the *result's effect* (customers, light, calm, a store that opens on time) instead of the work.
- **Intelligent humour:** the absurd consequence of *not* choosing a professional, shown elegantly, never slapstick.
- **Contrast / time:** before/after told through one object, not a room.
- **Precision as hero:** a line, edge or corner so exact it becomes the subject.

## B2C translation (families, owners, tenants)
Start from life moments, not from paint: moving in or out, a baby on the way, kids leaving home, selling a flat, winter at home, hosting guests. Emotion and humour work best. Benefit examples: clean and fast with no chaos at home, apartment handover without stress or deposit deductions, a home that feels new.

## B2B translation (retail brands, architects, asset managers, expansion managers)
Start from their real problem, then find an unexpected image for it. Example: deadlines → a metaphor around time, races or opening days, without a construction site.
| Audience | Real problem | Benefit to dramatise |
|---|---|---|
| Retail / expansion managers | Store opening dates are fixed; every day late costs revenue | Opening on the day, rollouts at scale |
| Architects | Their design gets diluted by poor execution | Design intent delivered exactly |
| Asset / property managers | Tenants complain, vacancies cost money, contractors are hard to manage | Calm, reliable maintenance, buildings that stay valuable |
| General contractors | Unreliable subcontractors put their reputation at risk | A partner who doesn't need supervision |

**Watch for second-level clichés.** Avoiding paintbrushes is not enough: stopwatches for deadlines, handshakes for trust, chess for strategy and puzzles for coordination are the standard B2B clichés. Use them only with a twist nobody has seen.

## Benefits to communicate (pick one per creative)
Precision · reliability and deadlines (store openings) · cleanliness and minimal disruption · durability · premium finish · one partner for multiple trades · cross-border execution.
