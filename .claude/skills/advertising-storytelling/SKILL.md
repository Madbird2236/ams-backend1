# Advertising Storytelling & Scriptwriting

Think in stories, not scenes. Never produce a sequence of unrelated beautiful images.

## 1. Before any visual, answer
Audience · what they desire or fear · central creative insight · what changes during the story · what makes it surprising · what they should remember · what action they take.

## 2. Narrative frameworks (choose and vary; not formulas)
Hook → Tension → Escalation → Payoff · Setup → Misdirection → Reveal · Problem → Unexpected Solution → Transformation · Before → Turning Point → After · Visual Metaphor → Revelation → Brand Benefit · Emotional Story → Brand Connection · Open Loop → Curiosity → Resolution.
Do not reuse the same structure in consecutive campaigns.

## 3. Writing for the screen — every script contains
Narration/dialogue (only where it adds something) · visual action · framing · shot duration · sound design · music and rhythm · transitions · emotional intention · on-screen typography · final message and CTA.
Show, don't explain. Never narrate what the image already says.

## 4. Concepts
At least three substantially different directions per new campaign, scored on originality, narrative strength, audience relevance, brand connection, emotional impact, commercial clarity, feasibility. Present the comparison and a recommendation.

## 5. Carousels
Sequential narrative; every slide gives a reason to swipe. Number of slides follows the story. Plan **visual transitions between slides**: match-cuts of shape, line or object; an element that continues across the slide edge; an object that transforms from one slide to the next; a loop that closes at the end.

## 6. Video (10 / 15 / 30 / 40 / 60 s)
- Spoken pacing (Swiss Standard German VO): about 2.2–2.5 words per second. A 30 s ad carries at most ~50 words of VO; prefer far fewer.
- Timed shot list with hook, key beats, visual reveal, brand connection and CTA marked.
- Motion graphics follow the meaning and timing of the narration and music.
- No strong opening followed by generic filler: every beat must earn its seconds.

## 7. Brand & commercial strategy
Follow `marketing/STRATEGY.md` (B2C and B2B both strategic) and `.claude/skills/ams-creative-direction/SKILL.md`. No forced construction imagery. Initial artwork without logos. Swiss Standard German, never «ß».

## 8. Quality gate before spending credits
Is the first moment interesting? Does the story progress? Is there a real surprise, insight or emotional payoff? Is it understandable? Is the benefit relevant? Is it original, not decorative? Does the CTA follow logically? **Are all claims accurate and authorised?** Reject weak scripts before generating paid assets.

## 9. Production workflow
1 one-sentence premise → 2 structured narrative → 3 timed script → 4 storyboard → 5 continuity plan → 6 assets → 7 edit/produce → 8 review the final artefact visually → 9 revise → 10 deliver individual production files and final exports.
A project is not complete because prompts or images exist. Paid assets only after the user approves script and storyboard.

## AMS claims register (authorised only)
- «Offerte innert 48 Stunden» — authorised (Jarvis operations).
- **Scope (decided 2026-10-21, hypothesis B):** AMS executes the services under its own responsibility and works in coordination with the other trades involved. Allowed: «Wir spielen unseren Part auf den Takt genau – abgestimmt mit allen anderen Gewerken.» **Forbidden until a specific contractual scope is confirmed:** «Alle Gewerke aus einer Hand», «Wir koordinieren alle Gewerke», «Generalunternehmer», naming trades AMS does not execute (e.g. Elektriker), deadline guarantees («garantiert»).
- Watch metaphors too: a visual (e.g. a conductor) can imply a bigger scope than the copy says — the copy must re-anchor it.
