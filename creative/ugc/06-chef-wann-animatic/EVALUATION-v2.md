## Critical comparison
**Better:**
- The joke reads silent: lift → stamp → press back; drumming; finger. A viewer without sound follows every beat.
- The structure now has a real climax: tension (three refusals) → release (one long, uninterrupted movement). The end card arrives as a consequence, not as an add-on.
- Removing the last line made the film quieter and more confident. It feels less like a sketch and more like a brand film.

**Still weak:**
- **The satisfaction is only promised.** Flat SVG cannot deliver the physical pleasure of a real peel (paint ridge, light, micro-texture). The macro proves the timing and the placement; it does not prove the feeling. That needs real footage or a very good generation.
- The low-angle finger shot is clear but the drawing is crude; in live action this beat lives on body language (he does not turn, he keeps painting).
- At 18.7 s it is 1.3 s longer than v1. Every second now carries a job, but a final cut could test 16–17 s by trimming B2.

**Is it memorable?** The mechanism is now: the viewer waits, laughs at being refused, then is rewarded with exactly the thing they wanted to see, and the reward *is* the slogan. That is ownable and different from construction ads. Whether it becomes memorable depends on two production facts: comic performance of the refusal, and a truly perfect macro edge.
