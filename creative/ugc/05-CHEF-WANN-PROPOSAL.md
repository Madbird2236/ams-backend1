# «Chef, wann?» — creative proposal (no generation, 0 credits)

Status: proposal for Adriano's decision. The 18-credit Seedance test is **not** authorised.

## 1. Honest diagnosis of concept C as first written
C v1 (apprentice asks, master explains, crisp edge, «Siehst du?») is better than a presenter monologue, but it is **not original**. «Meister erklärt dem Lehrling» and the satisfying tape pull are both established TikTok/Reels genres in the trade. A viewer has seen this video before. It would be a good tutorial, not a memorable AMS piece. Score honestly: Surprise 2.5.

## 2. The idea that makes it ours
**Insight:** in painting, bad work rarely comes from lack of skill. It comes from impatience: tape pulled too early, the second coat too soon, filler sanded before it is dry. The master's real skill is knowing **when**.

**Idea in one sentence:** the video makes the viewer wait for the satisfying moment, exactly as the master makes his apprentice wait, so the structure of the video *is* the message.

**Mechanism:** a running gag («Noch nicht.») plus withheld gratification. Everyone who watches tape-pull videos wants the pull; we deny it three times. The impatience the viewer feels is the insight, felt rather than explained. The pull finally comes, and it is the apprentice's hand that does it, guided. Mastery shown as transmission, not as performance.

Why this is not a construction ad: the subject is patience and judgement, a human trait, not walls. The work is the setting, not the message.

## 4. Why it can become an asset, not a one-off
The last line opens a **series**: «Chef, wann?» Each episode is one timing question from real craft (tape, second coat, filler, silicone, airing after a mould treatment), always the same POV, the same «Noch nicht.», the same payoff structure. A recognisable format is something a competitor cannot copy without looking like a copy. It also produces content without new ideas each week: low marginal cost per episode.

Audience: B2C (private clients) and employer branding (apprentices, skilled staff). **Not** a B2B rollout piece: «Noch nicht» next to retail deadlines would send the wrong signal; B2B keeps its own language.

## 6. Risks and open questions (to decide before any credit)
1. **The payoff is exactly what AI does worst.** Two hands interacting, tape physics, a perfectly straight edge. If the edge looks wrong, the whole joke collapses and a painter will spot it instantly. Craft details must be technically correct (permanent rule).
2. **«Chef» inside an AMS video implies a real AMS boss.** A fictional AI «Chef» would effectively pose as AMS staff, which our rule forbids. Only two honest options: (a) Adriano's own character element, labelled «KI-generiert», with his consent; or (b) real footage.
3. **Apprenticeship claim:** the piece suggests AMS trains apprentices. Does AMS actually have Lernende? If not, the second character must be a young colleague, not a «Lernender», and employer branding is off the table.
4. **Technical rule:** «angetrocknet, langsam, ca. 45°» is the manufacturer-aligned v3 (tesa/3M); tape and paint types differ, so the line stays a craft habit, not a universal law.
5. **Narration vs image (rule 6g):** «Langsam. Schräg.» must match what the hands do on screen.

## 7. Recommended path
1. **Free:** a local animatic (storyboard frames, real timing, the «Noch nicht» rhythm, sound) to prove the gag works before anything else. If the waiting is not funny in the animatic, it will not be funny generated.
2. **Strongest option: film it for real** on an AMS site with a phone (≈15 min, two people, no actors). This is the format's natural home: authentic UGC, no AI label, no hand/tape risk, real proof of AMS craft. AI then stays for ideas that cannot be filmed.
3. **If AI is preferred:** the 18-credit draft tests only beats 1–2 (characters, German speech, comic timing). It does **not** test the payoff, which is the main risk. Better: a hybrid, AI for beats 1–3 and a real phone close-up for the tape pull.
