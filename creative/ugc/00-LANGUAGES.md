# Four audiovisual languages — what each one is for

| | Cinematic advertising | Editorial explainer (Vox-style) | Motion graphics | **UGC (presenter)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Promise to the viewer | «Feel this» | «Understand this» | «See this clearly» | «Trust me, I do this every day» |
| Main tool | Story, image, sound, emotion | Voice leads, visual evidence, maps, charts | Type, shapes, icons, rhythm | A person, a tip, a proof shot |
| Look | Crafted, graded, composed | Paper, collage, highlights | Designed, branded | Phone-recorded, natural light, imperfect |
| Text on screen | Little, cinematic | Labels, headlines, sources | Central | Discreet subtitles only |
| Best for AMS | Brand, B2B trust, campaigns | Explaining process/scope (B2B, education) | Offers, steps, data, end cards | B2C tips, trust, social reach, recruiting |
| Kills it | Cheap effects | Decoration without evidence | Clutter | Effects, polish, ad-speak, a wrong craft detail |

Rule: choose the language by objective. **Never turn UGC into an explainer full of effects.**
