# Calibration 04 — Versatility: B2B retail fit-out — study, no credits

## Brief (same for all three)
- **Audience:** retail brands expanding in Switzerland: expansion and store-development managers, store-design leads, retail operations. Shopping-centre managers are a secondary audience.
- **Commercial objective:** qualified rollout conversations (B2B contracts). CTA: «Rollout-Gespräch vereinbaren.»
- **KPI:** qualified meetings, tender invitations, meeting → offer in AMS OS.
- **Scope (claims register, hypothesis B):** AMS executes painting, drywall/Gipser and retail fit-out under its own responsibility, coordinated with all other trades. Allowed: «abgestimmt mit allen anderen Gewerken». Forbidden: «alle Gewerke», «Generalunternehmer», naming trades AMS does not execute.
- **Guardrails:**
  - No «Offerte innert 24 Stunden» (B2B).
  - Promise words (am geplanten Tag, pünktlich, jede, garantiert) only with proof or a softer frame.
  - Never name or imply real brands. Blocklist respected: no TSG, Viega, SIXT, Arc'teryx, Copenhagen.
- **Lessons applied:**
  - Each idea needs its own logic.
  - Give on-screen text its reading time.
  - Add human emotion.
  - Use transitions that change meaning.
  - Measure contrast.
  - Avoid the conductor, which is already ours.
  - Avoid B2B clichés: stopwatch, handshake, chess, puzzle, relay baton.

**Buyer insight.** An expansion manager is judged on three things nobody sees in a render:
1. Does store no. 7 look exactly like store no. 1?
2. Did the work disturb the centre and the neighbours?
3. Can they trust a partner they rarely meet on site?

The emotional driver is **control from a distance**.

## A — Direct advertising · logic: the buyer's own document as proof standard
**Idea in one sentence:** Every retail brand has a store manual; AMS's promise is to execute it faithfully, store after store.

- **Headline:** «Wie im Store-Manual. Filiale für Filiale.»
- **Sub:** «Maler-, Gipser- und Ladenbauarbeiten für Retail-Rollouts in der Schweiz – abgestimmt mit allen anderen Gewerken.»
- **CTA:** «Rollout-Gespräch vereinbaren»
- **Visual (19/10 template, B2B tone):** top-down flat lay. On the left, an open store manual page:
  - a technical detail drawing of a wall corner with dimensions;
  - a colour field;
  - a material swatch.

  On the right, a photo of the executed corner, identical down to the shadow gap. A thin dimension line runs across both and connects them.
- **Why it converts:** it speaks the buyer's language (manual, detail, standard) and turns «quality» into something checkable. It qualifies the audience by itself: only people who own a store manual feel addressed.
- **Pre-flight:**
  - ✓ «Filiale für Filiale» describes a method, not a guarantee.
  - Re-checked with the calibrated claims rule (2026-10-22): «In jeder Filiale.» would also be a legitimate advertising expression (evaluative, not a measurable commitment). Both work; «Filiale für Filiale» keeps the rhythm of a rollout.
  - ✓ Scope B wording.
  - ✓ No 24 h.
  - ✓ No brand shown: the manual is a fictional, neutral one.
- **Scores (discounted):** Surprise 3 · Clarity 4.5 · Ownability 4 · Premium 4.5 · Feasibility 5.

## B — Conceptual advertising · logic: unexpected witness (humour about invisibility)
**Idea in one sentence:** The best fit-out in a running centre is the one nobody notices, so the only witness is the night guard.

- **Headline:** «Der Einzige, der uns gesehen hat.»
- **Sub:** «Ladenbau im laufenden Betrieb: sauber, leise, abgestimmt mit dem Center-Management.» (night/weekend work confirmed 2026-10-22)
- **CTA:** «Rollout-Gespräch vereinbaren»
- **Visual:**
  - A shopping centre at night, empty.
  - A security guard stops his patrol; his torch lights up a brand-new, flawless storefront. It is still behind a half-raised shutter, with no logo on it.
  - His expression is half impressed, half puzzled: yesterday this was a construction site.
  - Nothing else in the mall shows that work happened.
- **Why it works:**
  - The benefit (minimal disruption, clean handover) is shown through its effect, not the work.
  - The human face carries the joke.
  - Centre managers, a secondary audience, recognise their own fear.
- **Pre-flight:**
  - ✓ «im laufenden Betrieb» and night work confirmed by Adriano (2026-10-22).
  - ✓ No cliché.
  - Culture: a Swiss centre (signage in German, CHF prices in neighbouring windows).
- **Scores (discounted):** Surprise 4 · Clarity 4 · Ownability 3.5 · Premium 4 · Feasibility 4.

## C — Cinematic storytelling (31 s including a 4.5 s end card; corrected from «27 s») · logic: one detail travels through Switzerland (match-cut journey, the client as protagonist)
**Idea in one sentence:** An expansion manager wonders whether five stores in three language regions can feel like one; one physical detail, matched from city to city, answers the question.

| Time | Picture | Text / VO | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | Head office, late. The expansion manager (40s, tired) looks at a map of Switzerland with five pins. Close-up on her thumb on the last pin | VO (her, inner): «Fünf Städte. Drei Sprachen. Ein Standard?» | Clock-less silence, rain on glass |
| 4–8 s | Close-up: her finger traces the manual's detail drawing, a wall edge with a shadow gap | — | Paper |
| 8–18 s | **Match cuts on the same detail.** A hand runs along the finished edge in Zürich → the same move, the same edge in Bern → Basel → Lausanne → Lugano. Each cut changes only the light and the language on the hoarding: «Eröffnung», «Ouverture», «Apertura» | — | One musical phrase carried through every cut, each city adding one instrument |
| 18–24 s | She visits the fifth store. She runs her own hand along the edge, stops, smiles: she can't tell which city she is in | — | The phrase resolves |
| 24–27 s | The doors open; first customers; her phone shows a photo from the team: «Bereit.» | — | Room tone, a soft chime |
| End card | «Fünf Städte. Eine Handschrift.» / «Maler, Gipser, Ladenbau – abgestimmt mit allen anderen Gewerken.» / CTA «Rollout-Gespräch vereinbaren» | — | — |

- **Why this logic:**
  - The client is the hero, and AMS is the hand that never appears as a logo until the end. Emotion: relief from control at a distance.
  - The transitions change meaning: the same detail stands for the same standard across cities.
  - It shows AMS's ambition (multi-region, later Europe) without claiming it.
- **Pre-flight:**
  - ✓ Romandie and Ticino coverage confirmed (2026-10-22): the five-city, three-language journey is accurate.
  - «Eine Handschrift» is a metaphor for a consistent standard, not a guarantee.
  - The end-card sub keeps the scope B wording.
  - Reading time: the end card has 2 lines and 2.5 s minimum, so the card holds 4 s.
- **Scores (discounted):** Surprise 3.5 · Clarity 4 · Ownability 4.5 · Premium 5 · Feasibility 2.5 (multi-location look, cast, consistent detail; AI video possible but costly).

## Ideas I discarded (and why)
- **Countdown / stopwatch to opening day:** the standard B2B cliché.
- **Relay race, the baton passed between trades:** a 2nd-level cliché, and the baton is already the conductor's.
- **Puzzle pieces forming a store:** a cliché, and «puzzle» implies AMS assembles all trades, which is a scope A risk.
- **«Ihre ersten Kunden campieren schon.»:** exists already; it also carries an unproven on-time promise (retro finding N1).

## Which approach for which objective
| Objective | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified meetings now (LinkedIn, targeted by job title) | **A, direct** | Speaks the buyer's language; self-qualifying; easy to back with case photos |
| Recognition and talk among retail ops and centre managers | **B, conceptual** | Human face and humour; shareable; positions «no disruption» |
| Premium positioning, pitches, website, multi-region ambition | **C, storytelling** | Emotion plus proof of standard; works as a pitch film; highest cost, so later |

**Recommended system:**
1. A runs first, because B2B buyers need proof before poetry.
2. B supports reach.
3. C becomes the pitch film once AMS has real rollout footage. Real footage is better than AI here, because B2B buyers check authenticity.

## Self-critique after Adriano's feedback on calibration 03
- **Silent test:**
  - **A passes.** The manual and the executed corner, joined by a dimension line, say «faithful execution» without words.
  - **B half passes.** Without the headline it is «a guard sees a shop at night»; the *invisibility* of the work is not yet visible. Stronger cue (not produced): the folded construction hoarding on a trolley beside the finished store, still labelled «Hier entsteht …», and the guard's coffee steaming.
  - **C** carries its meaning through the edit (the same edge in five cities), so it works without words.
- **Claims rule:** «Eine Handschrift.» and «Filiale für Filiale» are advertising expressions and stay. «im laufenden Betrieb» and multi-region coverage are **service facts**, so they stay ⚠ until confirmed.

## What I learned from repeating the exercise
- In B2B the strongest emotion is **relief** (control, no surprises), not laughter. Humour works only when it shows a professional virtue (B).
- Scope discipline shaped the ideas: I avoided every metaphor that implies «we do everything» (puzzle, conductor, relay).
- Answered 2026-10-22: night/weekend work in operating centres yes; Romandie and Ticino yes; real rollout photos yes.
  - A should use real photos instead of AI images, after checking each client's permission to show the store.
