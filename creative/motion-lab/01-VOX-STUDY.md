# Study — the editorial explainer language (Vox-style), adapted to AMS

Goal: understand why the language works and adapt its principles. **Do not copy Vox's identity:** no Vox yellow, logo, fonts or signature layouts. AMS uses its own palette (brand blues, warm neutrals) and typography.

## 1. What the language is
An explainer where the **voice-over leads** and every visual is evidence for the sentence being spoken. It looks like a researcher's desk come alive: maps, photos cut out of paper, documents, charts and handwritten-feeling marks, arranged in layers and explored by a slow virtual camera.

## 2. Principles and why they work
| Principle | What it looks like | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **One idea per shot** | Each 2–5 s beat shows one fact; the next beat adds one more | Working memory holds little; stepwise disclosure keeps the viewer with you |
| **Voice leads, picture proves** | The image appears *as* the words say it, or a beat after | Hearing + seeing the same thing doubles comprehension and trust |
| **Show the source** | Documents, screenshots, archive photos, with the key line highlighted | Credibility: «this is real, not our opinion» |
| **Hierarchy by highlight** | Most of the frame is calm and desaturated; one element is highlighted, circled or coloured | The eye goes where the explainer wants, in under a second |
| **Collage with depth** | Photo cut-outs with a white or paper edge, soft shadows, on textured paper; layers move at different speeds | Tactile and «made by hand» feels honest; depth adds production value cheaply |
| **Maps as story** | Start wide (a country), zoom to the place, labels appear, paths draw | Gives context and scale; a moving map is a story of «where» |
| **Charts that build** | Axes first, then data drawing on, then one annotation that states the point | The viewer sees the pattern form before being told it |
| **Expressive headlines** | Short titles, big, sometimes typed or stamped, structuring the video into chapters | Signposting: the viewer always knows where they are |
| **Transitions that follow the logic** | Zoom into a detail to go deeper; pull out to go broader; slide along a line to go forward in time | The camera move *is* the argument's direction |
| **Sound as glue** | Light music bed, paper and pen sounds, small clicks on each appearance | Rhythm and tactility; it makes the stepwise reveal feel alive |
| **Calm pace, then accent** | Mostly slow push-ins; rare fast moves at turning points | Contrast makes the key moment land |

## 3. Anatomy of a typical beat (3–5 s)
1. The VO sentence starts.
2. A new element enters (cut-out, map label, chart bar) with a small sound.
3. The camera drifts slowly towards it (push-in 3–5 %).
4. On the key word, the highlight or circle draws on.
5. Hold half a second for reading, then the next beat.

## 4. Typography in the language
- Two families: a strong sans for headlines and labels, and a mono or typewriter face for «documents» and data. AMS: Inter Display + a mono, or Geist where the brand needs it.
- Labels are short (1–4 words), placed next to what they name, with a thin leader line.
- Numbers are big and simple, units small.
- Text stays on screen for at least its reading time (≈ 0.3 s per word + 0.5 s).

## 5. Colour and texture
- A neutral paper base (warm off-white or dark board), desaturated photos, **one accent colour** for highlights.
- AMS accent: brand blue #3FA3D6, with #1E293B for text. Never a yellow highlighter.
- Texture: subtle paper grain and soft shadows; nothing glossy.

## 6. Where it fits AMS (and where it doesn't)
| AMS use | Why this language |
|---|---|
| **B2C education**: why mould grows in winter (warm humid air, cold surface, condensation) and what AMS does (removal and surface treatment) | Explains without lecturing; builds trust; keeps AMS's role accurate |
| **B2C process**: from request to visit, offer within 24 h, execution, handover | Takes away uncertainty; one beat per step |
| **B2B rollout**: map of Switzerland, three language regions, cities, night work in operating centres | Shows real capability as evidence (confirmed facts only) |
| **B2B case study**: store manual → detail → executed photo (with permission) | «Show the source» is exactly what B2B buyers want |
| **Not for**: emotional brand films, humour sketches, fast social hooks | The explainer is calm and rational; use cinematic or comic languages there |

## 7. Risks
- **Too slow for social:** the hook must still come in the first 1–2 s (a bold question or a surprising map move).
- **Decoration:** paper texture and highlights without a point become a style exercise. Every element must answer the VO.
- **Invented evidence:** documents and numbers on screen must be real, or clearly illustrative. In B2B never fake a case.
